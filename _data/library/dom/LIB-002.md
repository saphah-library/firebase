TITLE: "Amen and Its Pagan Roots — Exposing the Deception of Ammon-Ra | DOM6027 Library"

## Content

### Thesis and Purpose
This article presents an alternative reading to recent popular scholarship: the liturgical use of the word "Amen" as a standard prayer-ending is argued here as potentially connected to syncretism with the Egyptian deity Amun / Ammon-Ra. For restorationist communities seeking to centralise worship on YHVH, this article recommends replacing "amen" with the Hebraic blessing Baruch YHVH.

### Historical and Cultural Background
The name Amun (Amon / Ammon) was central in Egyptian religion and later syncretised with Ra as Amun-Ra. Through regional cultural exchange, linguistic and ritual elements spread across the ancient Near East.

The phonetic similarity between “amen” and “Amun” is noted in this interpretation as a possible area of cultural overlap, though linguistic transmission is complex and layered.

### Spiritual Concerns and Liturgical Caution
From a pastoral perspective, any ritual term that resembles or echoes a deity-name should be treated carefully. This article argues that continued unexamined use of "amen" may carry unintended historical associations.

### Liturgical Recommendation
Replace "amen" with:
- Baruch YHVH (Blessed is YHVH)

Suggested practice:
- Leaders end prayers with Baruch YHVH
- Congregation responds with Shalom or Scripture refrain instead of “amen”
