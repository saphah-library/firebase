TITLE: "Paleo-Hebrew Basics: Rediscovering the Ancient Tongue of Scripture"

## Content

### Overview
This study introduces Paleo-Hebrew as the ancient pictographic script of Israel and explores its relevance for restoring covenant understanding and scriptural interpretation.

### Nature of Paleo-Hebrew
Paleo-Hebrew is a pictographic alphabet in which each letter carries both phonetic and symbolic meaning. It predates the later square script and reflects early Israelite writing traditions.

Examples include:
- **Aleph (𐤀)** — Ox head; strength, leadership, beginning
- **Bet (𐤁)** — House; family, dwelling, covenant space
- **Shin (𐤔)** — Teeth; consuming, transformation, fire imagery

### Symbolic Reading
Words formed in Paleo-Hebrew can be read both phonetically and conceptually, producing layered theological meaning that reinforces covenant concepts.

### Historical Usage
Paleo-Hebrew appears in ancient inscriptions, seals, pottery, and early manuscript traditions, including pre-exilic Israel and early scriptural fragments.

### Scriptural Context
Torah commands regarding inscription and remembrance (e.g., Devarim 6:9) were originally expressed within this scriptural environment.

### Restoration Purpose
Studying Paleo-Hebrew is presented as a restoration practice:
- Reconnecting with ancient covenant identity
- Deepening understanding of Scripture
- Recovering layered meaning lost in translation

### Practical Steps
- Learn the Paleo-Hebrew alphabet and symbols
- Practice writing key Torah verses
- Integrate symbolic meanings into study and devotion

### Conclusion
Paleo-Hebrew study is framed as a restoration of memory and meaning, reconnecting believers to early covenant expression and strengthening scriptural engagement.
